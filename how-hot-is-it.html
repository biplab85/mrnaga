The naga ghost pepper by numbers

Once the hottest
chilli on earth.

Mr Naga is made with the naga ghost pepper, known in Bangladesh as naga morich and to the rest of the world as Bhut Jolokia, the ghost pepper.

  • Scoville rating

    1,041,427

    Top of the range. The naga rates between 855,000 and 1,041,427 SHU.

  • Versus jalapeño

    ~200×

    Roughly two hundred times hotter than the jalapeño on your nachos.

  • Versus habanero

    ~3×

    About three times the heat of a habanero, the usual benchmark for "very hot".

  • Guinness World Record

    2007–11

    Held the record for the hottest chilli on earth for four years running.

Scoville scale comparison

Where the naga sits.

From capsicum to Pepper X. The naga lands among the true superhots, just below the modern record-breakers.

What that means in the jar

Mr Naga is
a pickle.

A Scoville rating measures the chilli, not the product.

In Mr Naga the naga is cured with oil, salt and spices, which changes how the heat arrives: slower, rounder, carried on flavour rather than delivered as a spike. It is still properly hot. It is just not a dare.

Start with a quarter teaspoon. You can always add more.

  1. ¼tsp

    Noticeable warmth

    The smoke comes through first. A good place for anyone new to the naga.

  2. Most popular ½tsp

    Properly hot

    The amount most people settle on. Real heat, with the flavour still in front.

  3. 1tsp

    Memorable

    A dish people will talk about afterwards. Have the yoghurt ready.

  4. Neatspoon

    Genuinely painful

    Straight from the spoon. We do not recommend it, and we know some of you will anyway.

Heat with flavour underneath

Flavour

“Smoky, slightly fruity, almost sweet at the edges.”

Plenty of hot products get their heat from extract. That gives you burn and nothing else. The ghost pepper has real flavour underneath the heat, and pickling it whole keeps that flavour in the jar.

  • SmokyThe signature of the naga. It sits under everything else in the dish.
  • Slightly fruityA bright, ripe note that keeps the heat from tasting flat.
  • Almost sweet at the edgesRounded off by oil, salt and pickling spices, so the heat arrives slowly.

Safety precautions

Handle with respect.

It is a food, and a very good one. It is also a superhot chilli. A few sensible rules.

  • Keep it out of reach of children. Store the jar up high, lid on, in the fridge once opened.
  • Wash your hands with soap and oil. Not just water. Capsaicin is oil soluble, so water only moves it around.
  • Do not touch your eyes. And do not touch anyone else's. Wash first, every time.
  • Check with your health first. Not recommended if you have a sensitive stomach, reflux, or a heart condition.
